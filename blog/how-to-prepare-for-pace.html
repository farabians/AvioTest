How to Prepare for the PACE Psychometric Exam

By AvioTest Editorial • January 20, 2026 • 6 min read

The PACE (Pilot Assessment & Selection System) is one of the most rigorous psychometric evaluations used by major airlines, including Turkish Airlines. For many aspiring pilots, this exam represents the most significant hurdle in their recruitment journey. Understanding its structure and preparing effectively is the key to success.

What is the PACE Exam?

The PACE assessment is designed to evaluate a candidate's mental capacity, cognitive skills, and personality traits to determine their suitability for the cockpit. Unlike traditional academic exams, PACE focuses on how you process information under pressure, your spatial awareness, and your multi-tasking abilities.

"Success in psychometric testing isn't about being a genius; it's about familiarity, speed, and consistency."

Key Modules to Master

The PACE exam consists of several critical modules, each testing a specific skill set:

  • Mathematics & Physics: Basic principles applied to aviation scenarios.
  • Spatial Orientation: Understanding your position in 3D space using instruments.
  • Audio Visual Memory: Recalling information presented through multiple channels simultaneously.
  • MIC (Joystick Test): Hand-eye coordination and motor skills assessment.

Preparation Strategies

To excel in the PACE exam, we recommend the following approach:

1. Start Early: Don't wait for your invitation letter to start practicing. Give yourself at least 4-6 weeks of consistent daily training.

2. Simulate Real Conditions: Use training tools like AvioTest that replicate the actual interface and difficulty levels of the PACE system. Practice in a quiet environment without distractions.

3. Analyze Your Performance: Pay attention to the modules where you consistently score lower. Focus your efforts on these weak points while maintaining your proficiency in others.

Final Tips for Exam Day

On the day of the exam, ensure you are well-rested. Psychometric tests are mentally exhausting, and fatigue will significantly impact your reaction times and memory. Stay calm, read every instruction carefully, and don't panic if you make a mistake—keep moving forward.
